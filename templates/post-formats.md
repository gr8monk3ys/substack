# Essay formats that work on Substack

Structures to reach for when the blank page wins. Each is a shape, not a formula —
the personality is still yours to supply.

Use with: `./substack.py post new "Working title"`

---

## 1. The Reversal
The highest-performing essay shape for a personality-led publication.

1. State the belief everyone holds (fairly — no strawman)
2. Concede exactly what's right about it
3. The turn: the thing it misses
4. The cost of getting it wrong
5. What to do instead

*Works because:* disagreement generates replies, and replies are the top-weighted
signal in the 2026 Notes ranking. Fair-then-reversed reads as thinking, not
contrarianism-for-its-own-sake.

---

## 2. The Confession
1. Something you did badly, in specific detail
2. What you believed at the time
3. What it actually cost
4. What you'd tell yourself now
5. The general principle — arrived at last, never announced first

*Works because:* personality brands run on disclosure. Nobody subscribes to a
person who has never been wrong.

---

## 3. The Close Read
Take one thing — a paragraph, a scene, a decision, a line of code — and go
absurdly deep on it.

1. Present the artifact
2. The obvious reading
3. The thing nobody notices
4. Why that small thing is actually the whole thing

*Works because:* depth is the clearest possible signal that you're not
generating filler. One of these outperforms five overview posts.

---

## 4. The Field Notes
1. A numbered list of observations from a period of doing something
2. No connective tissue — let them sit next to each other
3. Close by naming what they have in common, or refuse to

*Works because:* scannable, quotable, and every entry is an independent Note
you can pull out later.

---

## 5. The Case Against Yourself
1. State a position you hold
2. Build the strongest possible argument against it
3. Say honestly whether you survived it

*Works because:* almost nobody does it, and it buys more trust than any amount
of confidence.

---

## 6. The Origin
1. The moment it started
2. What you thought it was about
3. What it was actually about
4. Where it left you

*Works because:* narrative is the one format that survives being about
"anything" — the throughline is you.

---

## Structural rules, all formats

- **The first two lines decide everything.** Inbox previews and the app feed
  truncate. No throat-clearing, no "in today's edition".
- **Cut the first paragraph.** It's almost always warm-up. Delete it and check
  whether anything was lost.
- **One turn per essay.** The paragraph where the reader's mind changes. If you
  can't point at it, you've written a list of observations.
- **Write one restackable line on purpose.** A single sentence someone can
  screenshot. Don't leave it to chance.
- **End on a question.** Comments are a ranking signal and your best source of the
  next three posts.
- **Title is a promise, not a label.** "On Attention" is a label. "You Are Not
  Distracted, You Are Avoiding Something" is a promise.

Run `./substack.py post check <draft>` before you publish.
